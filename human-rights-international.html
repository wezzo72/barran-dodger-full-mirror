International examination · A receipt is not a verdict

International human rights

The testimony is offered to anyone who will read it: special procedures, disability-rights bodies, whistleblower organisations, researchers, and the public. It is not offered as a finding those bodies have already made.

The Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights gave the urgent appeal the receipt number UR/UST/23/AUS/17. The same number is kept on this site at UR/UST/23/AUS/17. That number proves a submission was received. It does not prove the United Nations adopted the account, granted asylum, or condemned a government.

The asylum claim filed with that history is the Barran Dodger asylum claim. The examination already on this site is the asylum investigation. A person still inside Australia is not, on the face of Article 1A(2) of the Refugee Convention, a refugee by that article alone, because the article requires the person to be outside the country of nationality. That limit is stated so a reader is not misled. The humanitarian facts of the life are not cancelled by that legal limit.

The instruments

The treaties named on this archive include the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, and the Convention against Torture. Naming a treaty is not a ruling that a state has breached it. It is the standard the testimony asks to be read against.

A further communication can be considered through UN Special Procedures. This page does not claim that a new communication was lodged today.
