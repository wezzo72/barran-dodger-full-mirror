12. Evidentiary method: how each claim should be examined

I ask every recipient or reviewer to apply a transparent method to each material proposition.

Category 1 — Official record. A decision, letter, report, or other record issued by a government body, tribunal, regulator, or other identified institution. The original should be checked for authenticity, date, author, scope, and context.

Category 2 — Documented fact. A proposition directly established by a reliable document or independently verifiable record.

Category 3 — First-person testimony. My account of what I experienced, saw, heard, reported, or understood. It should be preserved accurately and assessed in context.

Category 4 — Allegation or complaint. A claim submitted for investigation that has not yet been established by an independent finding.

Category 5 — Inference. A conclusion drawn from one or more facts. The reasoning and alternative explanations should be stated.

Category 6 — Interpretation or opinion. My analysis of the meaning, motive, or significance of events. It should be identified as analysis rather than silently presented as an official finding.

Category 7 — Spiritual belief or prophetic writing. Material expressing my religious, philosophical, or prophetic understanding. It may be important to understanding my work and personal meaning, but it should not be misrepresented as a judicial or scientific finding.

Category 8 — Unresolved question. A matter for which the current record is incomplete, disputed, or insufficient to support a definitive conclusion.

I ask that a reviewer not erase a claim merely because it remains unresolved. The correct response is to record the claim, identify the supporting material, state what remains unknown, and determine whether further inquiry is warranted.
