SOCIAL ISOLATION AND INSTITUTIONAL NON-RESPONSE Dr Richard William McLean — writing publicly as Barran Dodger Abstract This paper examines a central question arising from the documentary archive of Dr Richard William McLean, publicly known as Barran Dodger: how can a living person remain physically present while progressively becoming functionally invisible to the institutions upon which recognition, protection, housing, healthcare, disability support, financial security, legal assistance, investigation and public accountability depend?

The argument advanced here is not that every person named in the archive participated in a single conspiracy. The stronger and more testable proposition is that functional erasure can arise through the interaction of multiple ordinary institutional mechanisms: fragmentation of responsibility, repeated referral, administrative categorisation, credibility discounting, dependence upon gatekeepers, procedural exhaustion, failure to investigate, institutional silence, social isolation, economic dependency, serviceprovider power, inaccessible legal remedies, reputational degradation and the cumulative consequences of decisions that may individually appear minor but collectively produce extraordinary deprivation.

The distinction is critical. A person does not have to be physically imprisoned, legally disappeared or formally declared nonexistent to experience what may be described analytically as functional erasure. Functional erasure occurs when the person remains alive but progressively loses practical access to the systems capable of recognising the person, hearing the person’s account, preserving the person’s evidence, correcting institutional error and providing an effective remedy.

The documentary corpus maintained in the public Barrandodger repository provides an unusually extensive source base for examining this proposition. The repository contains dedicated evidence, source, email, statement, official-document, forensic-report, legal-brief and archival structures, alongside hundreds of individually named documents. Its README expressly states that original evidence is to remain preserved and that the underlying records are not to be altered. (GitHub)

The appropriate conclusion is therefore not that every allegation in the corpus has already been proved. Rather, the archive creates a substantial documentary basis for an independent investigation into whether repeated institutional decisions and omissions, across different domains and over time, produced a cumulative condition in which a living person could become increasingly invisible to the very systems charged with recognising and protecting him. ⸻

1. Introduction: From physical existence to institutional existence The fundamental problem documented by the Barran Dodger archive is not simply whether one particular complaint was accepted or rejected. 10. The seventh mechanism: legal exhaustion A further mechanism is procedural exhaustion.

An individual can theoretically possess rights while practically lacking an effective mechanism through which those rights can be exercised.

The distinction between formal availability and practical accessibility is critical.

A legal remedy may exist on paper while being inaccessible because of cost, eligibility, representation requirements, procedural complexity, jurisdictional fragmentation, disability-related barriers, time, exhaustion or institutional refusal to recognise the underlying complaint.

11. The eighth mechanism: referral loops One of the most difficult mechanisms to detect is the referral loop.

Institution A directs the person to Institution B.

Institution B says the matter belongs to Institution C.

Institution C says it is outside jurisdiction.

Institution D treats the matter as a complaint against Institution A.

Institution A then relies upon the existence of Institution D as evidence that the matter has already been dealt with.

The person moves around the administrative system while never reaching an institution capable of determining the whole factual matrix.

This mechanism is especially important when allegations cross organisational boundaries.

A complaint concerning disability support may involve a service provider, a government department, a financial administrator, police, health services, housing, legal assistance and oversight bodies.

12. The ninth mechanism: omission rather than action Functional erasure does not necessarily require a positive act.

A letter is not answered.

A warning is not escalated.

A complaint is redirected.

A record is not reviewed.

A request for investigation receives no substantive determination.

A person asking for protection receives another procedural instruction.

A service provider fails to respond.

An oversight body declines jurisdiction.

A political office acknowledges correspondence without producing intervention.

No single omission necessarily appears decisive.

Collectively, however, omissions can create a closed system in which nothing happens.

13. The tenth mechanism: mobbing and distributed responsibility The archive contains a document explicitly entitled “MOBBING-AND-THE-SAFETY-OF- THE-CROWD. ” (GitHub) This introduces an important sociological possibility.

Large-scale institutional harm does not necessarily require hundreds of people consciously deciding to harm one person.

Person C relies upon Person B.

Person D assumes someone else has investigated.

Person E treats the person’s escalating distress as evidence that the complaint is unreliable.

Person F closes the matter.

The result may be devastating even though no single participant possesses the entire picture.

The same principle applies to conspiracy.

22. Institutional self-protection The strongest systemic hypothesis may not be that every participant personally wanted the author’s destruction.

Consequently, an institution can acquire an incentive to defend the previous decision.

The original error becomes the premise of the next decision.

The next decision becomes institutional precedent.

The precedent becomes organisational truth.

The person challenging the original decision therefore becomes the problem.

This mechanism is capable of producing severe injustice without requiring every participant to be malicious. ⸻
