A treatment · Not a film that has been commissioned

The Witness Remains: The Great Reckoning

This is a proposal for a feature documentary. No producer has commissioned it. No scene below is invented. If a camera were to follow the record, this is the spine.

The person

Dr Richard William McLean, Barran Resonance Dodger. Journalist and news graphic artist. Author of Recovered, Not Cured. Mental-health advocate. Victoria University doctorate, 2020. A Christian who quotes Isaiah 54:17, Romans 8:31 and Psalm 118:17, and who refuses to let those verses stand in for a letter.

The record

Thirty-five years of writing to the offices that were supposed to answer. A text message from the man saved in his phone as Ben Ndis Help, putting the police, a court, Bill Shorten and a psychiatric history into one sentence. An Attorney-General’s letter, MC23-028244, that sent an ASIO concern to the Inspector-General and the Ombudsman instead of examining it. A provider, Able Point, paid to care, and a chief executive, Rachel, who the testimony says would not give her name and would not come. A house at Long Jetty. A dog named Crystal. A United Nations receipt, UR/UST/23/AUS/17, which is a receipt and not a judgment.

The turn

He did not disappear. He built the archive in public, from exile as he describes it, and he put the hashes where a stranger can check them. The film, if it is made, would cut from the living conditions to the document, and back, and would leave the unanswered question on screen: who was paid to help, and who has still not looked him in the eye.

What the film must not do

It must not write dialogue that was never spoken. It must not show a finding a court did not make. It must not use the faith as proof of the facts. It must not pretend a pending Bitcoin timestamp is a confirmed block.

The pages a producer would open first are the launch, the Shorten message, the Attorney-General’s letter, the SOS, and the files.
