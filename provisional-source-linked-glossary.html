Provisional source-linked glossary

The command forbids a final glossary before the source examination can support it. This page is not that glossary. It records only terms whose definitions were actually read in batch 1, and it keeps them at the status the opened page will bear. No underlying PDF was opened. A later batch can add, split, or remove any entry.

The author’s earlier vocabulary is not deleted. It is not treated as the examination’s conclusion. The batch 1 narrative · The coverage register · The lexicon · The command

Entries grounded in pages that were read

Non-allocation

TERM. Non-allocation

CATEGORY. administrative

DEFINITION. The lexicon defines it as a written decision by an oversight office not to allocate a public-interest disclosure for investigation.

TECHNICAL MEANING. In public-interest disclosure practice, non-allocation is a decision not to send a disclosure on for investigation. The precise legal test depends on the Act and the decision-maker. This batch did not re-state a section number from an unopened Act.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The lexicon page, read in batch 1. It names a Federal Court PID assessment of 27 March 2023, an Ombudsman item of 22 March 2023, 18 June 2023, and a statement to the NACC of 3 July 2023. Those files were not opened in this batch. The stage-1 report, also read, discusses a 27 March 2023 Federal Court letter in the same terms and says the letter was read on 11 November 2026. This batch did not download it again.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. A page says an office decided not to allocate a disclosure. The letter itself was not re-read from the PDF.

PATTERN. Named on more than one already-published page. Repetition across pages is not yet repetition proved from the originals.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Not inferred. A jurisdictional or statutory limit is one possible explanation. A refusal to look is another. The file has to decide which description fits.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. Not re-established in this batch beyond the pages’ statement that the process did not go on to investigation.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. The decision may be a lawful limit of the office’s power. The lexicon itself says non-allocation does not prove the disclosure was false and does not prove a crime.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. The underlying letters were not opened here, so this batch cannot show a passage that contradicts the lexicon. Absence of that check is the counter-weight.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Prior analytical vocabulary on the lexicon. Not a formal finding. Not adopted as this examination’s conclusion.

CONFIDENCE. Low, as a finding. The definition was read on the page named. The underlying file was not opened in batch 1. This entry can be withdrawn or changed when that file is read.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. Public-interest disclosure legislation may be relevant. The section was not re-read from the statute in this batch.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. The term is warranted as an item that must be tested, because the lexicon defines it narrowly and ties it to named files. It is not warranted yet as a proved description of those files.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. Open the 27 March 2023 Federal Court letter and the other files the lexicon names, and see whether each one is a decision not to allocate.

Service restriction

TERM. Service restriction

CATEGORY. administrative

DEFINITION. The lexicon defines it as a limit placed by the Commonwealth Ombudsman on how the complainant may approach the office.

TECHNICAL MEANING. Oversight bodies sometimes set contact conditions. Whether a particular notice is a service restriction, and on what grounds, is a question for that notice.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The lexicon page, read in batch 1. It names a file it calls “Service restriction, 20 January 2026”. That file was not opened.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. Not observed from the original notice. Observed only as the lexicon’s description.

PATTERN. One named notice on the lexicon. Not yet shown to be a series.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Not inferred.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. Not re-established from the notice.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. A restriction can be a response to volume or conduct, or an unfair barrier. The lexicon says the notice proves a restriction was notified and does not, by itself, prove the complaints were vexatious or true.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. The notice was not opened, so no contrary sentence was read.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Prior lexicon vocabulary. Not a finding of this batch.

CONFIDENCE. Low, as a finding. The definition was read on the page named. The underlying file was not opened in batch 1. This entry can be withdrawn or changed when that file is read.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. The Ombudsman’s own statute and service policy would be the place to test it. They were not opened.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. Warranted as a question, because a named date and office are given. Not warranted as a concluded classification.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. Open the 20 January 2026 notice and record the exact restriction, the reason given, and any review offered.

Fatal-injury notice

TERM. Fatal-injury notice

CATEGORY. evidentiary

DEFINITION. The lexicon defines it as an automated email of 1 July 2022 saying item IR8415987 had been opened, titled a report of a “fatal” injury in which a provider and participant was revived.

TECHNICAL MEANING. An automated open-item notice is evidence that a system recorded an open event and a title. It is not a medical report.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The lexicon page, read in batch 1. File named: IR8415987. Not opened in this batch.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. Not observed from the email. The lexicon states the title and the date.

PATTERN. Described as one notice, not as a series.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Not inferred. An automated title can be a classification error, a quotation of someone else’s words, or an accurate label. The email and the report would have to be compared.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. The lexicon does not treat the notice as proof of a medical outcome. This batch does not add one.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. The title may not match the contents. The lexicon says the notice names no hospital, no date of injury, and no cause.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. The email was not opened here.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Prior lexicon vocabulary about a named automated email. Not a clinical finding and not a finding about how any injury occurred.

CONFIDENCE. Low, as a finding. The definition was read on the page named. The underlying file was not opened in batch 1. This entry can be withdrawn or changed when that file is read.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. None determined in this batch.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. Warranted as a source-control question: what the subject line says, and what it does not say.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. Open IR8415987 and any medical report it points to. Do not treat the subject line as the report.

Provider and participant

TERM. Provider and participant

CATEGORY. disability

DEFINITION. The lexicon defines it as NDIS language for a person who was both a provider and a participant, and says the IR8415987 subject line uses both words.

TECHNICAL MEANING. Under the NDIS, provider and participant are different roles. One person can occupy both at different times or in different relationships. The subject line does not decide which role applied to which event.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The lexicon page. The same unopened item, IR8415987.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. Two role-words are said to appear in one subject line. The line was not re-read from the email.

PATTERN. One named subject line.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Not inferred.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. Not re-established.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. The words may describe the title of a form rather than a finding that he held both roles in the relevant incident.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. Email not opened.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Prior lexicon vocabulary. Not a determination of employment or of participant status.

CONFIDENCE. Low, as a finding. The definition was read on the page named. The underlying file was not opened in batch 1. This entry can be withdrawn or changed when that file is read.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. NDIS Act role definitions were not re-read in this batch.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. Warranted only as a warning not to collapse two roles into one conclusion.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. Read the subject line in the original email and any record that actually assigns a role.

Three determinations

TERM. Three determinations

CATEGORY. administrative

DEFINITION. The lexicon defines it as three offices writing about one working life and not reading each other: a Federal Court letter on employment, Comcare declining a claim under its own Act, and the NDIS Commission writing that its process did not make him its employee.

TECHNICAL MEANING. Different statutes ask different questions. Disagreement between them is not automatically a contradiction, and it is not automatically consistency.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The lexicon page names Tredwell, 8 January 2024; McLean and Comcare; and an NDIS service-failure notice of 20 January 2026. None of those three was opened in this batch. The stage-1 report discusses a different Federal Court letter, 27 March 2023, and was not used to fill this entry.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. Not observed from the three files.

PATTERN. The lexicon presents them as one set. That presentation was not checked against the files.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Not inferred. Separate statutory questions can all be answered without any office reading the others.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. Not re-established.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. The lexicon’s own limit: different statutory questions are not the same finding.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. Files not opened.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Prior lexicon vocabulary. Not a finding that the offices contradicted each other.

CONFIDENCE. Low, as a finding. The definition was read on the page named. The underlying file was not opened in batch 1. This entry can be withdrawn or changed when that file is read.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. Each Act’s employment or claimant test would have to be read with the letter that applied it.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. Warranted as a cross-reading task. Not warranted as a completed cross-reading.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. Open the three named writings side by side and record the question each one actually decided.

Administrative erasure

TERM. Administrative erasure

CATEGORY. administrative

DEFINITION. The lexicon calls this his term: a life recorded in many files and examined in none, each office stopping at the edge of its power.

TECHNICAL MEANING. Not used here as a settled term of art. The lexicon says the term is his map.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The lexicon page, which points to “Report 03, The Architecture of Administrative Erasure”. That report was not opened in this batch.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. Not extracted from the files. The map is the author’s.

PATTERN. Not assessed.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Ordinary jurisdictional limits can produce the same gaps without a design to erase. That alternative is open.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. Not re-established.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. The lexicon says the map is not a finding and is testable only by reading the files it names.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. Those files were not read in this batch. The stage-1 report’s discussion of “evidentiary erasure” is a different prior analysis of five letters, and this batch does not merge the two terms.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Author’s term, as defined and limited by the lexicon. Not an independent classification.

CONFIDENCE. Low. Not adopted.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. None determined.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. Included so the author’s term is not silently replaced, and so it is not treated as the examination’s conclusion.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. Read the files the map names and see whether each office stopped at a stated limit, and whether any office examined the substance.

Paradoxical inversion

TERM. Paradoxical inversion

CATEGORY. literary / analytical

DEFINITION. The lexicon defines it as the record turning around: the offices he says caused the detriment are the offices that wrote it down, as determinations, refusals, and closures.

TECHNICAL MEANING. Not a legal element. The lexicon calls it an argument about reading, not a verdict.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The lexicon page, pointing to The Documentary Trap and a question on the site. Those were not opened in this batch.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. Not re-observed.

PATTERN. Not assessed.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Offices writing down a refusal is ordinary record-keeping. It can coexist with harm. It does not, by itself, prove the harm.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. Not re-established.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. The lexicon’s limit: a letter still only proves what it says.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. Not tested against the letters.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Author’s analytical expression, preserved with the lexicon’s limit. Not a finding.

CONFIDENCE. Low. Not adopted.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. None.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. Included because the command requires the author’s vocabulary to be retained and distinguished from any later evidence-derived glossary.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. It would be confirmed only as a description of a reading method, and only after the letters are read together. It cannot be confirmed into a verdict by this batch.

Referral without an investigation finding

TERM. Referral without an investigation finding

CATEGORY. administrative

DEFINITION. A descriptive phrase for the reproduced letter MC23-028244: the Department apologises for delay and tells the correspondent he may take a concern about ASIO to the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security and other Commonwealth concerns to the Commonwealth Ombudsman.

TECHNICAL MEANING. Portfolio referral and suggestion of a complaint pathway. Not, in the text read, a determination of the complaint.

EVIDENCE IDENTIFIED. The reproduction of MC23-028244 on /the-government-record and /evidence-archive, read in batch 1. Original PDF not compared.

OBSERVABLE CONDUCT. The reproduced text refers, apologises for delay, and offers two complaint routes. It does not record a finding on the conduct.

PATTERN. One letter, reproduced on two pages. Not yet placed in a series by this batch.

POSSIBLE MECHANISM. Ordinary portfolio allocation is a sufficient explanation of this letter on its own. Whether later offices then investigated is a separate question and was not opened.

HUMAN CONSEQUENCE. Not established beyond the letter. The page’s further claim, that those offices were then closed or restricted, is not part of this entry.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. The letter may sit in a longer chain in which an investigation did occur. That chain was not read.

COUNTER-EVIDENCE. The gloss on the page goes further than the letter. That gloss is the counter-weight to over-reading this entry, and it remains untested.

EVIDENTIARY STATUS. Text of a letter as published on the site. Not independently authenticated against the original in this batch. Not a formal finding.

CONFIDENCE. Moderate that the reproduction, as read, is a referral and not an investigation finding. Low for anything about what IGIS or the Ombudsman later did.

LEGAL OR PROFESSIONAL RELEVANCE. The Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security Act and the Ombudsman Act may define those offices. Their texts were not re-read here. Naming them is not a finding that a duty was breached.

WHY THIS TERM IS WARRANTED. The phrase is warranted because it stays with the words that were read and refuses the stronger gloss.

WHAT WOULD CONFIRM OR DISPROVE IT. Compare the reproduction with the original PDF. Then open whatever IGIS and Ombudsman records exist after 19 September 2023.
