17 November 2026. This list replaces the shorter register of the same day. The command is saved separately. This page is the index. It is not a court finding.

Key terms and phrases

The command is saved in full. The names below were taken from the letters the site quotes. They were not taken from a list supplied in the command. A file that is only named is not treated as read.

What was opened, and what was not

Opened as the text published on this site: the official records page, where each letter below is quoted; the FOI file; the Attorney-General letter on the plan page; the bank outcome; Ben’s messages; the letter to police; and the SOS. The underlying PDFs were not re-opened beyond the sentences those pages print. The gospels, the essays, and the rest of the public pages were not opened for this list. They are not described as empty.

Strength words mean only this. Directly established: the office’s own sentence is printed on the page. Alleged: a person is reported to have said it, and no police or court finding was opened. Named, not read: the operative sentence was not in the text that was opened. Author’s account: the page states it as the writer’s position.

1. Portfolio referral without an investigation

A letter sends the correspondent to another office and does not decide the complaint. On 19 September 2023, A Riley, Attorney-General’s Department, MC23-028244, answered the email of 5 July 2023 to the Prime Minister. It names ASIO, the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Security, and the Commonwealth Ombudsman. It does not investigate. Directly established as a referral. The same letter is printed on the plan page.

2. Delay acknowledged, then the matter sent on

The same letter says “I apologise for the delay in response,” and the next step is another address, not a finding. Directly established for this letter.

3. An enquiry that has not yet closed the file

On 5 August 2021 Ben Calder of the Victorian Ombudsman wrote that the office could consider the Mercy Health and Mental Health Complaints Commissioner matters under the Ombudsman Act 1973, that it could not give legal advice or representation, and that he would make enquiries. The letter. Directly established. It is not a closure.

4. Closure because further work is said to have no practical outcome

On 5 October 2021 the Victorian Ombudsman wrote that it was “not justifiable to continue,” that there were “insufficient grounds” for more enquiries, that “I have closed your case,” and that further correspondence “may not be responded to unless it raises new issues.” The outcome. Directly established. A purpose to harm is not written. The consequence is that the complaint ends.

5. Complaint records placed outside the FOI Act

On 14 June 2022, FOI/22/14, the Victorian Ombudsman apologised that a notification link had failed and declined to release complaint records because section 29A of the Ombudsman Act means the FOI Act does not apply. The refusal. Directly established.

6. A disclosure held below the statutory threshold

The Commonwealth Ombudsman, replying to the email of 10 November 2021, wrote that the information did not meet section 26 of the Public Interest Disclosure Act, and that the office would not communicate further on that disclosure outside the review process. The notification. Directly established.

7. Not an authorised internal recipient

On 16 September 2022 and again on 22 March 2023 the Commonwealth Ombudsman wrote that it was unable to progress the matter as an internal disclosure because it is not an authorised internal recipient under section 34. The 2022 letter also says the Act does not apply to the alleged conduct of state agencies and private entities. 2022. 2023. Directly established. The consequence is that those claims are not investigated under that scheme.

8. A threatened limit on contact, separate from the disclosure decision

The IBAC file of 18 November 2021, PID-2021-400008, says the disclosure did not satisfy section 26, and that if abusive comments continued “we may have to limit your contact with us in future.” The file. The limit is a condition. It is not the reason the disclosure was rejected.

9. Not treated as a public official

On 18 July 2023 the Department of Social Services wrote that it would not allocate the matter, because a public interest disclosure can only be made by a public official and it had no record of employment. The decision. The National Disability Insurance Agency, on the emails of 18 April 2023, wrote: “I have decided not to allocate the handling of your disclosure,” because the officer was not satisfied he was a public official, and “I will not take any further action in relation to the emails.” The allocation decision. Directly established. The consequence is that the disclosure is not investigated.

10. Not this department’s conduct

The Department of Health and Aged Care, on the disclosure of 19 October 2023, decided under section 44(3) not to allocate it, because it was not made to an authorised recipient and the conduct did not relate to that department. The decision. Directly established.

11. A review that is agreed, and is not itself a refusal

On 15 December 2021 the NDIA agreed to a section 48 plan review and said it would make contact within 14 days. The letter. Directly established. No detriment is written in it.

12. Housing support found ineligible, and daily support approved

On 1 June 2023 Lead Delegate Kate P, participant 430938559, wrote: “You are not eligible for Specialist Disability Accommodation.” “Supported Independent Living (SIL): not suitable for SIL.” Drop-in daily-living support was approved. The stated reason is that the information did not meet the SDA criteria after Amy Cavenett’s report of 3 May 2023. The decision. Directly established. A purpose to harm is not written. The consequence is that SDA and SIL are refused.

13. A decision made late because of the volume of documents

The NDIA apologised that the allocation decision on the 18 April 2023 disclosure was not made within the 14 days the Act requires, because of the number of documents. The reply. The detriment in that letter is the delay. The letter does not itself refuse the disclosure.

14. A funded plan that records no stable accommodation

The plan starting 20 November 2025, participant 430938559, states: “This plan’s total funding amount is $366,406.69.” Plan-managed Core is $274,255.84. It runs to 19 November 2026. It records: “I am 52 years old and currently have no stable accommodation.” The plan. Directly established. The money is on the paper. The address is not.

15. Support locked to named providers

The same plan says plan management “must only be provided by WA South West Plan Managers,” and specialist support coordination “must only be provided by Diversitas WA.” Directly established. The consequence is that the money and the coordination are confined to those providers.

16. A wide request read down to one department

On 14 April 2022 Petra Gartmann, Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, refused FOI/2022/045. The request of 7 February 2022 was “I request all information government agencies hold on me please.” The decision says the department does not hold other agencies’ records. Directly established. It does not establish what other agencies hold.

17. Refusal because the documents cannot be found or do not exist

The same decision refuses access under section 24A. That is a search outcome. It is not a finding about the person who asked. Directly established for this decision.

18. Extra time because a request is called complex and voluminous

On 23 March 2022 the Information Commissioner, RQ22/00794, extended the department’s time under section 15AB to 8 April 2022. The decision. Directly established.

19. A request deemed refused because it was out of time

On 22 April 2022, RQ22/01369, the Commissioner wrote that the department had not decided the request of 7 April 2022 in time, so the FOI Act deems it refused, and then gave further time to 14 April 2022. The decision. Directly established.

20. Identity-match uncertainty, then a partial release

The revised decision of 8 October 2024, FOI/2022/045IC, as printed on the FOI page, says the department told the Commissioner there was not enough information for a positive match, and that five documents were then released. Directly established as the page prints it. The five documents were not re-transcribed in this pass.

21. No power to confer whistleblower status

On 4 July 2023 the Australian Human Rights Commission wrote that it cannot “grant you whistleblower status,” and that being a whistleblower is not a protected attribute under the laws it administers. The letter. Directly established. The consequence is that the status is not conferred.

22. Unable to advise

The Office of the Official Secretary to the Governor-General, replying to the letter of 8 September 2024, wrote: “The Governor-General is unable to advise on this matter.” The reply. Directly established.

23. Emails filtered so they do not arrive

On 8 August 2025 Belinda, acting Senior Assistant Ombudsman, complaint 2024-706941, restricted contact for six months, to 8 February 2026. The stated reasons are 229 emails in twelve months, material outside jurisdiction or containing offensive images and abusive language, and a request of 3 January 2025 that was not followed. “Your emails will be filtered and held on receipt and will not reach the intended mailbox.” The restriction. Directly established. The consequence is that email to the office does not arrive.

24. Exclusion from the premises that administer the payment

On 19 March 2025 Services Australia, reference 305 689 451X, limited contact from 20 March to 31 July 2025 because of behaviour at the Tuggeranong Service Centre on 6 March 2025. He could not enter Centrelink, Medicare or Child Support premises or their carparks. If he did, “the police may be called and you may be prosecuted for criminal trespass.” The ban. Directly established.

25. Liability declined because the office is not satisfied he is an employee

On 26 May 2021 Comcare declined the workers’ compensation claim under the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988. The determination. Directly established. The email of 25 May is not that decision. It is the message before it.

26. The estate taken out of his control

On 16 October 2024 the NSW Trustee and Guardian certified, section 122(2), Matter 658238, that the Tribunal had committed management of the estate of Barran Resonance Dodger to that office. The notice. Directly established.

27. A vehicle disposed of to meet a budget

From 11 November 2025 the same office decided to dispose of the Ford Territory, registration S797CDP. The written purpose is “to help provide you with enough money to cover budgeted expenses.” The written reasons are that evidence of how the vehicle was obtained had not been provided, and that the trust account could not afford it. CF-15811059C, Reece, 2 and 3 November 2026, says NetBank was removed on 15 October 2026 outside the financial-management process and calls it a once-off staff error. The same outcome offers $500, to be accepted by 9 November 2026, tied to the complaint being resolved and to a release of further claims. The page says he did not accept. 31. A police file released, and then not readable

Victoria Police FOI 70120/20 is marked released, then “Not Relevant,” with exemptions under section 33(1) and section 31(1)(a). The file. The omission is the redaction. The pages read do not contain a narrative of an assault. That is not a finding that nothing happened, and not a finding that it did.

32. A title that says fatal injury and revival, which is not the medical chart

The Department of Social Services email of 1 July 2022 is: “Opened - [IR8415987] - Report of 'fatal' injury in which provider & participant was revived.” The file. Directly established as an opened-item notice. It is not a discharge summary and not a finding of death.

33. A reported close call, a reported payment, and a reported agreement not to speak

Ben’s messages print: “Yes even the police said it was a close call.” “The agencies went back through phone records and believed she was paid to discredit you.” “They made me sign a non disclosure agreement.” “I thought you were just paranoid. 34. An accusation that asks for the record and refuses to ask for the finding

The letter to police of 3 November 2026 says it is not asking the officer to find that an assassination was attempted or that any named person ordered one. It asks whether records are held, and it says an absence should be written down. Directly established as the text of the letter. The page says it had not been shown as lodged.

35. A present need stated by the person, not by an agency

The SOS asks for food, a safe room for him and for Crystal, a telephone, and removal from the place he says is unsafe. The bar of 10 November 2026 says no one has arrived. Author’s account of the present. No agency assessment of that day was opened.

36. A document that is not the decision it was indexed as

The OAIC item 43704714 is a privacy complaint form, not a decision. The Comcare email of 25 May 2021 is before the determination. The file of 11 April 2021 indexed as a CDDA rejection is the writer’s own email. The Small Business Ombudsman email of 15 October 2021 says a letter from Bruce Billson is attached, and that attachment was not in the text read. Named, and the operative sentence was not there.

37. Consequence, where a purpose is not written

Across the letters above, no office writes that its purpose is to harm him. What follows from the words, if they are carried out, is the consequence: the complaint closes, the email does not arrive, the status is not conferred, the housing is refused, the estate and the vehicle leave his control, the guardian decides where he lives and who his lawyer is, the disclosure is not investigated, the police pages cannot be read. That distinction is what the letters support. A secret order is not in them.

38. The sequence, and only the sequence

Put in time, the opened letters are an enquiry, then a closure, then refusals to allocate, then a search that says the documents are not there, then five documents, then a referral from the Prime Minister’s mail, then a clinical refusal of SDA and SIL, then a plan of $366,406.69 that records no stable accommodation, then a guardian, a trustee, a premises ban, a filtered ombudsman, a bank error, and an unanswered appeal for food and a door. Each item stands on its own letter. Together they are a pattern of decisions and non-decisions. They are not a single written order, and they are not a verdict.

What this page does not adopt

The gospels and the essays use the writer’s own names for the pattern, including administrative annihilation, civil death and social erasure. Those names are his vocabulary. They are not what these letters call themselves. The letters call themselves referrals, closures, ineligibility, restrictions and releases.

The letters are the evidence. This page is the map. The command. The official records.
