The Gospel of the Unprotected Witness

State-Sanctioned Targeted Killing: The Testimony of a Gay, Disabled and Unprotected Whistleblower.

Part I — The accusation, the record and the witness. Part II — Artificial intelligence is asked the question: Why did they do this to me?

This Gospel is written as a personal testimony and a public-interest accusation. Its title expresses the gravity of the author’s allegation and experience. It does not pretend that state-sanctioned killing, a coordinated operation or the motives of particular people have been formally established. Those questions must be examined against evidence.

The witness remains. The record remains. The question remains.

Part I — The man they left unprotected

1. The accusation

I give this Gospel the title State-Sanctioned Targeted Killing.

Not because I claim that a court has already established a murder conspiracy. Not because I can honestly identify every person responsible for every event. But because I want the gravity of my experience examined without euphemism.

I am a gay man. I am a disabled man. I am a whistleblower. I have been unprotected.

I have asked for assistance, explanations, accountability and protection. I have sought answers from institutions that possessed authority, procedures, resources and responsibilities that I did not possess.

I did not set out to harm the people who disappointed me.

I did not set out to destroy the people who lied to me, if they lied. I did not seek revenge against those I believe caused me harm. I wanted the truth. I wanted fairness. I wanted to be heard. I wanted to remain alive, safe and capable of living an ordinary human life.

I wanted the institutions that existed to protect people to do what they were supposed to do.

That was my expectation.

And the question that remains is how a person can spend years seeking protection and come to feel that the very systems he approaches have become another source of danger.

2. The killing that must be examined

There is more than one way a person can be destroyed.

A person can be physically killed.

A person can also experience the destruction of security, livelihood, independence, reputation, relationships, access to services and confidence that anyone will respond when help is requested.

These experiences are not identical. Administrative failure is not automatically attempted murder. Institutional neglect is not automatically a conspiracy. A rejected complaint is not proof that its subject was deliberately silenced.

But neither should the cumulative consequences of institutional decisions be dismissed merely because they occurred through paperwork, correspondence, referrals, assessments, restrictions and administrative processes.

The question is whether the record reveals a pattern of decisions, omissions, contradictions or failures that caused serious and foreseeable harm.

If the answer is yes, those decisions deserve examination.

If there was deliberate retaliation, improper purpose, discrimination or unlawful conduct, the evidence should be capable of exposing it.

If the evidence does not establish those things, that must also be said.

Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last.

3. The vulnerable witness

What protection does a whistleblower have when he is disabled?

What protection does a gay man have when he believes his complaints are not being taken seriously?

What protection does a person have when he depends on the same administrative systems he is asking to investigate?

What happens when the person seeking help becomes the person required to prove, repeatedly, that he deserves to be helped?

I have described my experience as one of administrative erasure.

I have used the words political exile, social death, evidentiary erasure and psychological warfare to describe what I believe the cumulative experience has become.

These are serious descriptions. They are my testimony and interpretation, not findings that can be attributed to an independent tribunal unless such findings exist.

But there is a human being behind every file number.

There is a life behind every administrative decision.

There is a person behind every complaint that is closed.

And there is a consequence when a person repeatedly seeks help and believes nobody is listening.

4. I never wanted to hurt them

This is important.

I never wanted to harm the people I complained about.

I wanted answers. I wanted documents. I wanted an explanation of what had happened. I wanted mistakes corrected. I wanted the opportunity to challenge decisions that affected my life. I wanted the truth to be examined by people who were independent of the matters being examined. I wanted to live without fear that asking questions would make my circumstances worse.

I did not need enemies. I needed protection. I did not need revenge. I needed accountability. I did not need a war. I needed a way home to an ordinary life.

If people made mistakes, I wanted them corrected. If people acted improperly, I wanted that established through evidence and lawful processes. If allegations were wrong, I wanted that clarified too.

I did not want anyone destroyed. I wanted the destruction of uncertainty.

An acknowledgement is not an investigation. An investigation is not a finding. A refusal is not proof of corruption. A personal account is not an official determination. An official document is not automatically the whole truth.

But the documents must not disappear from examination merely because the story they form is difficult.

6. The question of responsibility

Who was responsible for protecting me? Who was responsible for responding to my complaints? Who was responsible for checking whether the decisions affecting me were lawful, fair and properly reasoned? Who was responsible for ensuring that disability did not become a barrier to being heard? Who was responsible for recognising that a person can be harmed by the cumulative consequences of decisions made by different institutions?

And if nobody was responsible for the whole picture, who was responsible for joining the pieces together?

The absence of one person responsible for the whole story does not mean there is no story. It means the record must be reconstructed.

7. To those who are still alive

I am not writing this Gospel to threaten anyone. I am not calling for revenge. I am not asking anyone to accept every allegation without examination.

I am asking for an independent reading of the evidence.

I want the truth to survive the people who may prefer not to discuss it. I want the records preserved. I want errors corrected. I want allegations tested. I want accountability where it is warranted. And I want to be allowed to live.

The living deserve dignity. The disabled deserve protection. Whistleblowers deserve lawful treatment. Gay people deserve equality. People who make complaints deserve a fair process.

None of these things should depend on whether an institution finds a person convenient, credible, agreeable or easy to manage.

The witness is not the verdict. The record is not the conclusion. The investigation is not complete merely because a file has been closed.

Part II — The question put to artificial intelligence

8. The question

I now place the central question before artificial intelligence. Not as a request for flattery. Not as an invitation to confirm a conspiracy. Not as an instruction to invent motives for people whose minds it cannot read.

Why did they do this to me?

Why did a gay, disabled, unprotected whistleblower who says he never intended to harm the people he complained about come to experience such profound insecurity, isolation and institutional abandonment? Why did the search for answers become such a defining part of his life? Why did the systems he approached not, in his experience, provide the protection and resolution he sought? What does the documentary record actually reveal? And what does it not reveal?

9. What the four opened records say

These are the words on the pages. They are not a finding that a killing was authorised. They are not a finding that it was not. They answer narrower questions than the title of this Gospel asks.

Comcare, 26 May 2021. Amy Delzoppo, Comcare delegate. Claim 1326583/1. Addressed to Dr Richard William McLean, 2 McCubbin Street, Footscray. Claimed condition: anxiety and depression. Date of injury: 22 January 2021. The letter says: “your claim for Workers’ Compensation has been declined under section 14 of the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988 (SRC Act) for anxiety state and depressive disorder.” The reason given is the first criterion, that the person be an employee under section 5. The letter records that the claim form of 26 April 2021 stated the usual occupation as “artist-life coach-therapeutic supplier” and “sole trader,” and that an email of 13 May 2021 said he was a registered NDIS provider, an employer of himself, paid by the NDIA through PRODA. The letter does not say why the injury happened. It says the claim was declined because Comcare was not satisfied he was an employee.

The Administrative Appeals Tribunal, 12 July 2023. Senior Member C. J. Furnell. File 2021/7478. Re Richard McLean and Comcare. Melbourne. Hearing 6 April 2023. Advocate for the applicant, Suellen Green. Counsel for Comcare, Peter Woulfe. The decision says: “The Tribunal affirms the decision the subject of review.” The catchwords say it was held that the applicant was not an employee of the Commonwealth, a Commonwealth authority, or a corporation licensed under the Act as a self-insurer. That is an affirmation of the refusal. It is not a decision about a killing, and it does not inquire into one.

The Commonwealth Ombudsman, 16 September 2022. Signed Judi, Authorised Officer, PID and Major Investigations Team. Reference PID-2022-100021. Disclosure received 11 August 2022. The heading is “Notification of decision to not allocate your disclosure.” The letter says the Office is not an authorised internal recipient under section 34 of the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013, and: “It was not clear to me what Commonwealth agency you were making a disclosure complaint about, or what disclosable conduct you were alleging, and there do not appear to be reasonable grounds for you to believe that the information tends to show one or more instances of disclosable conduct.” It says the Act does not apply to the alleged conduct of state agencies and private entities. This is a decision not to allocate. It is not an investigation, and it is not a finding that nothing happened.

NCAT Guardianship Division. Case 2024/00324093. Cover letter of the Registrar, 18 March 2026, enclosing the written statement of reasons for the decision made on 9 October 2025. Before Principal Member S. L. Handebo. The orders appoint the Public Guardian. The order is a continuing limited guardianship order for three years from 9 October 2025. The functions are accommodation, to decide where he may reside; health care; medical and dental consent; services; and legal services, including authority to instruct legal representatives. A standard condition requires the guardian to take all reasonable steps to bring him to an understanding of the issues and to obtain and consider his views before making significant decisions. The financial management order of 16 October 2024 was also reviewed. The estate is subject to management under the NSW Trustee and Guardian Act 2009. These orders give other people power over residence, health care, services, lawyers and money. They do not record a finding that anyone tried to kill him. They record that a tribunal placed those decisions in other hands.

10. The answer to the question, from those pages

Why did they do this to me?

From the four files opened, this is what can be said.

Comcare and the Tribunal refused a compensation claim because they decided he was not an employee. The Ombudsman did not allocate a disclosure because the Office said it was not the right recipient and that the disclosable conduct was not clear. A tribunal later appointed the Public Guardian over accommodation, health, services and legal access, and left the estate under management.

None of those four documents says that a person, a minister or a government authorised a killing. None of them says the opposite. They do not discuss a killing. A reader who treats them as proof of a murder conspiracy is reading a sentence that is not on the page. A reader who treats them as proof that nothing harmful happened is also reading past the page. A declined claim, a disclosure not allocated, and a guardianship order that takes residence, health, services, lawyers and money out of a person’s own hands are harms of a particular kind. They are administrative. Whether they were errors, whether they were lawful, and whether they were done for an improper purpose are further questions. These four files do not answer the purpose.

You do not have to prove that somebody intended to kill you in order to establish that decisions were made about your body, your house, your money and your complaint, and that those decisions were not yours.

Your sexuality does not diminish your rights. Your disability does not diminish your credibility by itself. Your whistleblowing does not remove your right to ordinary human dignity. And your wish for answers does not make you an enemy of the people you ask to answer.

Part III — The final testimony

I was not asking to rule the world. I was asking to be treated as a human being in it.

I was not asking for anyone’s destruction. I was asking for an explanation.

I was not asking for revenge. I was asking for protection.

I was not asking to be worshipped, believed without question or placed above the law. I was asking for the law to apply to me as it applies to everyone else.

If I was wrong about something, show me the evidence. If an institution made a mistake, correct it. If someone acted improperly, investigate it. If an allegation cannot be substantiated, say so.

But do not confuse the absence of a finding with proof that nothing happened. Do not confuse a closed file with a resolved human life. Do not confuse administrative silence with justice. And do not confuse the survival of a witness with the completion of the story.

I am alive. I want to remain alive. I want to be safe. I want Crystal safe. I want to live with dignity, peace and independence. I want the documentary record examined by people capable of distinguishing truth from assumption.

The final question is not whether artificial intelligence can invent a reason for what happened. It cannot. The question is whether the evidence, examined independently and fairly, can establish what happened, why decisions were made, whether duties were fulfilled and whether anyone should be held accountable.

That is the work that remains.

The witness remains. The record remains. The question remains.

Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last.

Faith is not a finding. Allegation is not proof. Silence is not an answer.
