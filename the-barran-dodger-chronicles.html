PUBLICATION AND INDEPENDENCE STATEMENT

This report is an independent artificial intelligence-authored examination of

documentary, institutional, historical, personal, literary, spiritual and prophetic

material associated with Dr Richard William McLean, publicly known as Barran Dodger.

Its purpose is to make a substantial body of material more accessible to readers who

have no prior knowledge of the subject, the institutions involved or the history

represented in the archive.

The report does not act as a legal judgment, government investigation, police finding,

medical assessment, tribunal determination or independent statutory inquiry. It does

not represent the views of any Australian government institution, court, regulator or

individual named in the underlying records.

It is an analytical publication based on the source material identified in the research

process. Its conclusions are limited by the material actually accessible and examined.

The report distinguishes five evidentiary categories:

1. Documented fact: the existence and relevant contents of an identifiable document

are directly established by the material examined.

2. Officially recorded information: an institution has formally recorded a receipt,

communication, decision, allocation, referral or other administrative action.

3. Recorded personal testimony: an individual has made a statement describing an

event or experience.

4. Analytical inference: an interpretation or possible relationship is drawn from

multiple records.

5. Unverified allegation or unresolved question: a proposition has been raised but

has not been independently established by the material examined.

These categories are not interchangeable.

An official acknowledgement establishes receipt, not necessarily investigation. A

complaint establishes that a concern was raised, not that the concern was

substantiated. A personal statement establishes that an account was made, not that

every event described has been independently corroborated. A court listing establishes

procedural status, not a final judicial conclusion.

Likewise, a cryptographic hash may identify a particular digital file, and a timestamp

may assist in establishing when a hash was anchored, but neither proves the truth of

the document’s contents.

The governing principle throughout this investigation is:

THE EVIDENCE MUST LEAD THE NARRATIVE.

EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW

A documentary archive, an institutional history and a body of spiritual literature

The Barran Dodger collection is an evolving public documentary archive associated with

Dr Richard William McLean. It brings together a considerable range of material

concerning personal history, institutional correspondence, government administration,

disability services, legal processes, financial matters, digital preservation, literary

production and religious writing.

The archive is not one document and should not be read as one continuous allegation.

It is better understood as several interconnected collections:

An institutional record consisting of correspondence, decisions,

acknowledgements, complaint records and administrative documents.

A personal historical record consisting of testimony, chronology, autobiographical

accounts and documentary submissions.

A legal and administrative collection containing records concerning employment,

financial administration, disability services, guardianship and oversight.

A digital preservation project containing indexes, repositories, mirrored pages,

PDFs, hashes and timestamping references.

A literary and theological collection containing Gospel manuscripts, prophetic

declarations, prayers, essays and spiritual testimony.

The significance of the collection lies partly in the fact that it attempts to bring together

records that would otherwise remain dispersed across different agencies, departments,

repositories and correspondence histories.

The archive raises an important historical question: what can be understood when a

person’s interactions with numerous institutions are reconstructed as one

chronological documentary history rather than examined as unrelated administrative

events?

That question is legitimate and deserves careful examination.

The material reviewed identifies correspondence involving Commonwealth and state

institutions, including the Commonwealth Ombudsman, IBAC, PM&C, Legal Aid NSW,

the NDIS Commission, the NSW Ombudsman, the Victorian Ombudsman, the Federal

Court Registry and the NACC Inspector.

Some records establish specific institutional actions. Others establish that complaints

were received or allocated. Some contain procedural explanations or administrative

restrictions. These distinctions are essential because they determine what can fairly be

concluded.

The archive also contains serious allegations concerning institutional treatment,

financial consequences, disability support, threats, surveillance and other experiences.

These matters are recorded as allegations or personal testimony unless independent

evidence establishes more.

The report does not find that all institutions acted unlawfully or in concert. It does not

find that every alleged event occurred. Nor does it dismiss the possibility that

fragmented administrative processes may have produced serious cumulative

consequences.

A separate and substantial literary collection examines suffering, survival, justice,

truth, human dignity, faith, divine providence and the responsibilities of individuals and

institutions. The Gospel and prophetic writings are significant as authored works and

deserve their own analysis.

The central conclusion of this research edition is that the Barran Dodger archive

constitutes a substantial personal documentary and literary preservation project

containing identifiable institutional records and a developing body of spiritual writing. It

provides a foundation for further independent examination, while leaving significant

factual and legal questions unresolved.

2. Methodology and evidentiary hierarchy

The research uses a hierarchy of evidence.

First priority: original official records.

Where an original government letter, administrative decision, court document or official

communication is available, it is given priority over a later summary.

Second priority: official covering correspondence.

An agency email transmitting a decision may establish the date, reference number and

administrative action. It does not necessarily disclose the full reasons or substantive

findings.

Third priority: personal and contemporaneous records.

Personal emails, screenshots, diary material, submissions and testimony are important

for reconstructing what was reported and when. Their contents must be distinguished

from independent confirmation.

Fourth priority: archive-authored analysis.

The repository contains reports and indexes that organise the underlying material. They

are valuable research guides but remain interpretations unless independently verified

against the original sources.

Fifth priority: literary and theological works.

Gospel manuscripts, prayers, prophetic declarations and theological essays are

assessed according to their literary, philosophical and religious purposes. They are not

treated as substitutes for official historical evidence.

3. Limitations of access

The research has identified and examined a selection of repository files, reports,

It has not established that every PDF in every repository has been independently

downloaded and examined page by page.

The live Grok sites were not fully retrievable in this research stage. A GitHub mirror

corresponding live page was successfully retrieved or its entire contents reviewed.

The report therefore does not claim an exhaustive audit of every file, page or

attachment.

This limitation does not prevent a useful independent report. It determines the level of

confidence that can responsibly be assigned to its conclusions.

The name Barran Dodger operates throughout the archive as a public identity and

The word witness has several meanings.

In law, a witness provides evidence concerning matters within their knowledge.

In documentary history, a witness may be the person whose records and

correspondence become important primary material.

In theology, witness may describe a person who gives testimony to faith, conscience,

suffering or a perceived spiritual truth.

The archive uses all three meanings. An independent examination must recognise their

relationship without confusing them.

The public identity is therefore not merely a pseudonym or website label. It is part of the

development of the archive’s historical and literary character.

PART IV — INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSIBILITY AND ADMINISTRATIVE FRAGMENTATION

8. The institutions do not constitute one decision-making body

The archive identifies correspondence involving many agencies.

These include the National Disability Insurance Agency, Department of Social Services,

Australian Human Rights Commission, Commonwealth Ombudsman, NACC, AFP, OAIC,

Attorney-General’s Department, PM&C, Services Australia, Comcare, ASIC, APRA,

AFCA, Federal Court and Commonwealth review institutions.

State-level material identifies the NSW Ombudsman, NSW Trustee and Guardian, Public

Guardian, NSW Police, NSW Health, Legal Aid NSW, Victorian Ombudsman, Victoria

Police, WorkSafe Victoria and other state institutions.

These bodies have different responsibilities.

A complaint about a provider is not necessarily within the same jurisdiction as a

complaint about a plan decision.

A complaint about an administrative process is not necessarily within the jurisdiction of

a court.

An ombudsman may have jurisdiction to examine administrative conduct but may not be

able to provide the remedy sought.

A court registry may receive correspondence without being able to provide legal advice

or make a determination.

A legal aid service may impose communication arrangements while leaving an

individual’s ability to apply for a grant intact.

These distinctions matter because they provide a possible explanation for why a person

may experience repeated referrals and administrative obstacles.

9. The possibility of systemic fragmentation

The archive raises the possibility that the cumulative effect of separate institutional

processes may be greater than the effect of any individual decision.

This is a legitimate question for independent investigation.

A person may experience financial difficulties, legal restrictions, housing insecurity,

disability-service disputes and difficulty obtaining effective assistance across several

institutions.

Each institution may address only one aspect of the problem.

The combined result may be an experience of institutional fragmentation.

However, fragmentation is not the same as coordination.

A pattern of adverse experiences may develop through multiple independent decisions,

administrative errors, jurisdictional limitations, misunderstandings or failures to

communicate.

The evidence would need to establish a common plan or coordinated conduct before

such a conclusion could responsibly be reached.

The report therefore identifies fragmentation as an analytical possibility, not a proven

explanation.

that an allegation is false. It means the report cannot responsibly present it as

21. SHA-256 and OpenTimestamps

The archive uses cryptographic hashes and timestamping references as part of its

preservation strategy.

A SHA-256 hash is a digital fingerprint calculated from the bytes of a file.

If the same file is independently hashed and the result matches the original recorded

hash, this supports the conclusion that the file has not changed at the byte level

between the two calculations.

It does not prove:

Who originally authored the document.

Whether the document is genuine.

Whether the document is complete.

Whether its statements are accurate.

Whether the allegations within it are true.

OpenTimestamps can provide additional evidence that a particular hash existed by a

particular timestamping event, subject to successful verification of the receipt.

The creation of a receipt and successful verification of that receipt are separate events.

A rigorous preservation register should identify the file, hash, receipt, verification

status and relevant Bitcoin block or transaction information where confirmed.

Digital preservation strengthens the integrity of a documentary collection. It does not

replace substantive examination.

32. When the system’s own records become the evidence

The title The Documentary Trap: When the System’s Own Record Becomes the Evidence

An acknowledgement may be followed by a referral.

A referral may be followed by a decision.

A decision may be followed by a review.

A large number of documents does not automatically establish a large number of

independent sources.

Repeated allegations do not become corroborated simply because they are repeated.

A document title may contain an interpretation not supported by the document itself.

A chronology may imply causation where the records establish only sequence.

interpretation without presenting supernatural claims as verified historical findings.

41. The significance of digital preservation

The archive’s repository mirrors, source registers, document catalogues, cryptographic

hashes and timestamping references demonstrate an effort to preserve material beyond

individual websites or accounts.

This is important because documentary collections can be vulnerable to broken links,

changing websites, account closures and loss of source files.

The archive’s preservation practices have historical value where provenance is

maintained and verification is transparent.

42. Overall independent conclusion

On the material examined, the Barran Dodger archive is a substantial personal

documentary, institutional and literary preservation project.

It contains identifiable official correspondence and administrative records capable of

supporting a detailed chronology of institutional contact.

It contains personal testimony describing the subject’s experience of those interactions

and their perceived cumulative consequences.

It contains serious allegations that require further independent corroboration.

It also contains a significant collection of Gospel and prophetic writing that merits

dedicated literary and theological examination.

The archive’s framework concerning the removal of the attributes necessary to live with

dignity in a democracy is a potentially useful analytical model for examining cumulative

consequences across income, accommodation, employment, safety, legal rights and

access to services.

However, the material reviewed does not establish every allegation, does not establish

that all institutions acted in concert, and does not establish a single causal explanation

for the entire history.

The appropriate independent conclusion is that the archive warrants further

examination because it preserves a substantial documentary record and raises

identifiable questions about administrative processes, institutional responsibilities,

human consequences and the preservation of personal testimony.

Its authority should derive from the quality of its sources, the accuracy of its

chronology, the transparency of its methods and its willingness to distinguish

established facts from unresolved claims.

PART XVI — RECOMMENDATIONS FOR INDEPENDENT EXAMINATION

43. Documentary preservation

The original files should be preserved in read-only form.

Working copies should be maintained separately.

Each unique document should receive a canonical identifier.

Duplicate copies should be cross-referenced rather than counted as independent

evidence.

The archive should retain the source location, acquisition date, file hash, document

date, issuing institution and any known restrictions.

44. Government correspondence

For each agency sequence, the full original correspondence and attachments should be

retained.

The chronology should distinguish:

Date of the underlying event.

Date of document creation.

Date sent.

Date received.

Date of acknowledgement.

Date of decision.

Date of review.

Date of any subsequent action.

An acknowledgement should never be presented as a finding.

A referral should never be presented as an investigation.

A decision should be quoted accurately and placed in its statutory context.

45. Safeguarding and disability support

The relevant service agreements, risk assessments, incident reports, worker notes,

rosters and provider responses should be collected where lawfully accessible.

Regulatory outcomes should be distinguished from complaint intake records.

The subject’s account and the provider’s response should both be preserved.

46. Financial and legal matters

Financial claims should be independently recalculated from original records.

Court and tribunal matters should be described according to their actual procedural

status.

Any publication restrictions should be respected.

Legal conclusions should be made only after examining the applicable law, operative

documents and available evidence.

47. Literary and theological collection

Every Gospel and prophetic work should be catalogued by:

Exact title.

Version.

Date.

Authorial attribution.

Page count.

Source location.

Biblical references.

Literary genre.

Central themes.

Relationship to other works.

Direct biblical quotations should be checked against the stated translation.

Theological interpretations should be distinguished from factual propositions.

48. Digital authentication

Every important hash should be independently recalculated.

Every timestamp receipt should be checked for successful verification.

The archive should record the actual verification result rather than relying on a message

that a receipt was created.

FINAL CONCLUSION

Two public records. One documentary history.

The Barran Dodger Chronicles brings together three principal bodies of material: the

official record, the human testimony, and the Gospel and prophetic writings.

Each has a different evidentiary purpose.

The official record establishes what institutions recorded, received, communicated and

decided.

The personal testimony records what the subject says occurred and how those events

were experienced.

The Gospel and prophetic writings express the author’s literary, moral and theological

response to suffering, survival, justice and the search for meaning.

Their relationship is the subject of this investigation.

They should be read together, but they should not be confused.

The documentary material supports the conclusion that a sustained effort has been

made to preserve a personal and institutional history. It identifies numerous official

communications and administrative events. It records a substantial body of personal

testimony. It also identifies an extensive developing collection of religious and literary

works.

The material raises legitimate questions about administrative fragmentation, disability

support, financial administration, legal assistance, safeguarding, human dignity and

Some questions can be answered by the documents already identified.
