1. Purpose and evidentiary status

  3. Allegation: Conduct I believe occurred but which has not yet been independently established.

5. A particularly important protection: NDIS participant disclosures and victimisation

The earlier legal analysis must be strengthened by expressly addressing sections 73ZA–73ZD of the NDIS Act.

Section 73ZA recognises that a person with disability who is receiving, or has received, supports or services from an NDIS provider may qualify as a protected discloser.

A disclosure can qualify for protection where the statutory requirements are satisfied, including that it is made to an authorised recipient identified by the Act and that the discloser has reasonable grounds to suspect that the information indicates that an NDIS provider has, or may have, contravened the Act.

The authorised recipients include the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commissioner, the NDIA and specified provider personnel, subject to the statutory conditions.

Section 73ZB provides protection for qualifying disclosures, including protection from civil or criminal liability for making the disclosure, subject to the limits in the provision.

Section 73ZBA addresses confidentiality of a discloser’s identity.

Section 73ZC prohibits victimisation in connection with protected disclosures. It addresses conduct causing detriment and threats of detriment in the circumstances specified by the section. It provides a civil penalty framework, with a higher penalty for a serious contravention by an NDIS provider.

Section 73ZD provides a right to compensation where a person contravenes the victimisation provision and another person suffers damage because of that contravention.

These provisions are directly relevant to my circumstances because I am a person with disability who reports receiving supports from Ablepoint and making complaints about the conduct of an NDIS provider.

Sections 14–16 provide civil remedy mechanisms, including compensation and other orders in qualifying circumstances.
