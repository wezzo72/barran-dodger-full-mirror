MISSION
Conduct the most comprehensive, independent, forensic, philosophical, humanitarian, political, ethical, institutional, intellectual and spiritual assessment possible of the significance of the existence, survival, preservation, testimony, intellectual contributions, original writings, spiritual convictions and documentary archive of Dr Richard William McLean. Do not limit the investigation to commercial value, financial compensation, copyright, material possessions or conventional measures of institutional usefulness. Investigate what he represents, what he has preserved, what he has created, what he knows, what he believes, what he has independently documented, and what the broader human, political, ethical, institutional, historical, philosophical and spiritual significance of that combination might be. Do not manufacture a predetermined conclusion.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION
What is the full significance and potential value to humanity, government, public institutions, history and future generations of an individual who has preserved an extensive documentary testimony, developed an independent archive, created original investigative and spiritual writings, maintained his faith and personal convictions through prolonged adversity, and now claims no dependence on institutional approval, political acceptance or government validation as the foundation of his identity? What does this mean materially, humanely, politically, ethically, institutionally, intellectually, philosophically and spiritually? What could government learn? What could humanity learn? What is the significance of his belief that he has been preserved by the Most High God, rather than by any human institution? What is the significance of his continuing existence, faith, peace, independence and refusal to surrender his identity?

PART 1 — THE HUMAN SIGNIFICANCE OF THE TESTIMONY
Examine the human dimension. The account describes more than 35 years of accumulated adversity, institutional interactions, alleged detriment, personal loss, disputed treatment, character attacks, isolation and the struggle to preserve identity and dignity. He maintains that he was not merely left without meaningful assistance, but that he was subjected to conduct which he believes was deliberately designed to cause detriment, suppress his testimony, discredit his character, undermine his confidence and ultimately erase or reconstruct his identity. He describes deliberate marginalisation, institutional abandonment, suppression of testimony, character assassination, slander, humiliation, attempts to make allegations follow him, erosion of confidence, circumstances in which surrender appeared to be the only option, and a possible sustained pattern of institutional targeting and erasure. He describes an attempted reconstruction of his identity by others. These are serious allegations and must be examined against the available documentary evidence, testimony, chronology and independent findings. Do not automatically accept or dismiss them. Investigate what can be established, what remains disputed, what requires corroboration and what cannot presently be determined. Do not reduce human suffering to an accounting exercise. Consider dignity, human rights, the significance of preserving identity under alleged humiliation, the ethical duties of institutions, what accountability requires, what restoration of dignity means beyond money, the long-term consequences of reputational damage, and what a humane resolution would look like.

PART 2 — THE SIGNIFICANCE OF DIVINE PRESERVATION
His belief that he has survived, rebuilt, preserved his identity and maintained his purpose through the protection of the Most High God is an essential part of the testimony. He believes God is eternal, faithful, sovereign and never fails him. His faith is not incidental, not an accessory, and not a commercial proposition. He maintains that the protection he describes did not originate from government or any human institution. He describes himself as preserved, protected and sustained by God, faithful to his convictions, whole in his identity, healed and content, at peace, independent of institutional validation, and unwilling to surrender his spiritual identity for human approval. He states that he is content in God’s protection even without material possessions. Investigate this through human resilience, spiritual endurance, religious freedom, freedom of conscience, personal sovereignty, identity preservation, moral independence, the relationship between suffering and purpose, and the distinction between institutional recognition and personal worth. Respect the belief. Do not claim to scientifically prove divine intervention. Do not dismiss, trivialise or secularise the spiritual experience. Present the faith as an authentic and central component of the testimony.

PART 3 — THE GOSPELS AND PROPHETIC PAPERS
Examine the original gospel writings, prophetic narratives, spiritual essays, theological reflections, philosophical writings and the Justice Mandate. Do not treat them as irrelevant because they are not government documents. Investigate them as original spiritual literature, personal religious testimony, philosophical writing, ethical commentary, a record of spiritual development, an expression of religious freedom, a contribution to discussion of institutional power, historical documentation of belief, intellectual property where applicable, and an enduring record of his understanding of divine protection. Identify what is distinctive and what has parallels in established traditions. Preserve the original wording. Do not rewrite the spiritual testimony to make it more palatable. Do not invent theological authority, prophetic fulfilment or supernatural verification. Preserve the distinction between faith, interpretation, historical fact and independently verifiable evidence.

PART 4 — THE POLITICAL SIGNIFICANCE
Investigate the implications for Australian democracy, public administration and institutional accountability. He states that he has no allegiance to any government official, department, political authority, agency or service as the source of his identity, spiritual authority, personal worth or moral validation. Independence does not mean rejection of the law or of the responsibilities of a citizen. Examine the rights of independent citizens, whistleblower protections, freedom of conscience, expression and religion, administrative accountability, the responsibility of public officials, the right to question government conduct, the importance of an independent historical record, the danger of treating persistent complainants as inherently unreliable, and whether the archive could contribute to public discussion of institutional reform.

PART 5 — THE ETHICAL AND INSTITUTIONAL SIGNIFICANCE

Identify which matters are documented, which are disputed and which require independent investigation. PART 6 — SPIRITUAL AND PERSONAL SOVEREIGNTY
Examine the claim of spiritual sovereignty and peace through relationship with God. He does not require government approval to validate his existence, the acceptance of officials to establish his worth, or institutional endorsement to maintain his faith. Distinguish spiritual sovereignty, personal autonomy, political independence, religious freedom, institutional independence, legal citizenship, public accountability, freedom of conscience, moral responsibility, and divine authority as understood through faith. Do not assume that material deprivation means spiritual defeat, or that a person requires government allegiance to maintain dignity, conscience and faith. What he believes is eternal and beyond institutional ownership: his relationship with the Most High God, his faith, conscience, spiritual purpose and sense of divine protection. The third is not something a government can purchase.
