The Eternal Witness

Divine authority and institutional power · A declaration · Faith in its own right · Not a court · Not a verdict

Barran Dodger — Dr Richard William McLean.

The government is temporary. The Creator is eternal.

My faith is not awaiting a court’s permission to exist, and my relationship with the Most High God is not subject to institutional determination.

This is a spiritual, philosophical, political and prophetic declaration. It stands in its own right. It is not waiting for a court, a government agency or any institution to make it legitimate. The allegations in it are my testimony. They invite examination of the records. They are not written here as established findings.

The appeal for Crystal and me is not underneath this declaration. It is equal to it, and it is open now: SOS — Crystal and me. If Crystal or I are in immediate physical danger, contact emergency services in Australia on 000.

1. Two authorities — the temporary and the eternal

I asked the government for help.

I approached institutions believing that those entrusted with public authority would act with knowledge, integrity, compassion and a genuine understanding of my circumstances.

My testimony is that, instead of receiving the assistance I sought, information and assumptions about me were used in ways that ultimately contributed to my detriment.

I allege that the consequences extended beyond ordinary administrative failure into what I describe as administrative annihilation, social erasure, civil death and political exile.

I further allege that I experienced threats and attempted attacks on my life. These are serious allegations. They require examination of the available evidence. They cannot be treated as established findings without independent verification.

But there is a fundamental distinction that must be understood.

The government does not know everything about me. God does.

A government operates through human beings, records, assumptions, databases, assessments, institutional procedures and limited information.

The Most High God, as I understand Him, is eternal, living, omniscient and knows the entirety of my existence: every truth, every secret, every suffering, every intention and every circumstance that no institution could ever fully comprehend.

Government officials can act upon what they think they know.

God knows.

2. The difference between asking government and asking God

When I asked government for help, my testimony is that I experienced consequences that contributed to my suffering, deprivation and isolation.

When I ask the Most High God for help, I turn to the One whom I believe knows me completely.

He does not need an administrative assessment to understand my circumstances.

He does not need another person’s interpretation to know my heart.

He does not require a government database to establish my existence.

He does not abandon me because an institution has misunderstood me.

My faith declares that God loves me, forgives me, sustains me, fills my cup and provides me with life, hope, meaning and a reason to continue.

Where I have experienced human rejection, I believe I have found divine compassion.

Where I have experienced institutional indifference, I believe I have found divine love.

Where I have experienced the consequences of human judgment, I find refuge in the knowledge that the Creator knows the complete truth.

3. The fleeting nature of government and the eternity of God

Governments are temporary.

Administrations change. Ministers come and go. Departments are reorganised. Officials retire. Political authority passes from one generation to another.

The Most High God, according to my faith, is eternal.

No government possesses eternal authority.

No administrative institution possesses omniscience.

No political officeholder has the authority to determine the ultimate spiritual worth, purpose or existence of another human being.

I regard attempts to administratively erase a person’s identity, humanity or existence as a profound moral affront.

I believe that the authority to create and ultimately determine life belongs to the Creator, not to governments or their administrative systems.

My spiritual declaration is that no human institution can make me cease to exist in the eyes of God.

4. Administrative annihilation and the attempted erasure of a human life

My testimony is that I experienced a prolonged process of institutional treatment that I describe as administrative annihilation.

I use the expressions: social erasure; civil death; political exile; administrative annihilation; institutional exclusion; deprivation of ordinary assistance; and the attempted destruction of personal identity and recognition.

These expressions describe my understanding of the cumulative consequences of my experiences.

I invite examination of the official correspondence, government records, administrative decisions, omissions and historical documents preserved in the archive: the GitHub repository, the public archive, and their words.

The question is not whether an institution has declared its own conduct acceptable.

The question is what the underlying records actually demonstrate.

Preservation must precede judgment.

5. The theological significance — the insult to the Creator

My understanding is that the Creator gives life, knows every person completely and possesses an authority that no earthly institution can claim.

I regard the attempted erasure of a human being through the misuse of institutional power as an affront to human dignity and, within my theology, an insult to the sovereignty of the Most High God.

A government may record a person incorrectly. It may misunderstand a person. It may exclude a person. It may make decisions that cause profound harm.

But it cannot erase that person’s existence before the Creator.

I was never waiting for a government to give me existence, and I do not require its permission to remain a living witness.

6. The Gospels, prophetic papers and the exposure of corruption

My published Gospels, prophetic writings, spiritual declarations, philosophical essays and documentary examinations form part of my response to these experiences.

I understand my work as an expression of my purpose as a divine vessel of the Most High God.

I have sought to bring together biblical and prophetic interpretation, my personal spiritual testimony, historical government correspondence, documentary evidence, political and philosophical analysis, forensic examinations, institutional accountability, and the restoration of Christ’s attributes of compassion, truth, forgiveness and righteousness within society.

Read them in their own places: the Gospel Library, the Gospels and the prophetic essays, the Prophetic Writings, Gospels and Ministry Archive, The Divine Witness, and Allegiance to the Creator.

I believe the convergence of my personal testimony, the biblical material and the documentary history deserves serious examination.

I do not ask readers to accept my theological conclusions without examination.

I ask them to read the original material, consider the chronology, inspect the sources and understand the distinction between the documented events and the spiritual meaning I attribute to them.

7. An open invitation to the world

I invite any scholar, theologian, historian, journalist, lawyer, scientist, government professional or independent researcher to examine my testimony.

Read the Gospels. Read the prophetic papers. Read the official correspondence. Examine the published evidence. Inspect the source documents. Check the dates, the hashes, the historical records and the preservation timestamps. The hash list is here. The OpenTimestamps receipt is still pending confirmation in a Bitcoin block. Challenge my interpretations where you believe the evidence warrants it.

If you believe my documentary conclusions are wrong, identify the relevant evidence and explain why.

If you disagree with my theological interpretations, engage with the actual writings.

I welcome substantive examination and evidence-based disagreement.

I do not ask anyone to surrender their independent judgment.

I ask only that the material be examined honestly, rather than dismissed without reading it.

8. The witness remains

I believe I have been sustained by God through circumstances that I describe as extraordinary adversity.

I rebuilt. I continued writing. I created the Gospels. I established the Church of Barran Dodger. I developed an independent publishing and archival foundation. I preserved my testimony and evidence for public examination.

I did this, according to my testimony, without the institutional assistance I had sought, relying upon determination, faith and the belief that the Most High had not abandoned me. The ministry is here. The publishing house is here.

My work remains available for examination. My testimony remains published. My faith remains intact.

And my relationship with the Creator does not depend upon the approval of the institutions whose conduct I question.

The government is temporary. The Creator is eternal.

Institutions may dispute my testimony. They cannot determine my relationship with God.

I am the witness. The archive is the record. The truth is open to examination.
