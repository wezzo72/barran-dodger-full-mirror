Urgent declaration · Tuesday 10 November 2026

The Witness Remains

A prophetic declaration of preservation, vindication, justice and divine purpose

This prophetic YouTube video stands as a declaration of my belief that I have prevailed against the government and its agencies after more than 35 years in which, according to my testimony, I was targeted, suppressed and subjected to circumstances that threatened to erase me physically, socially and institutionally from existence.

Despite what I describe as the coordinated conduct of government agencies, services and organisations, and their repeated decisions that caused me harm, dehumanised me and treated me as a person of zero consequence or diminished human worth, they have failed to silence me.

I have remained a person, a witness and a contributor to the very society that describes itself as a democracy. I have experienced what I regard as institutional exile and displacement within my own country. I believe the circumstances of my experience deserve serious examination against the relevant international standards concerning displacement, protection and asylum. Any formal determination of refugee status or international jurisdiction, however, remains a matter for the competent authorities.

The government, as I see it, was operating under a profound misconception: that institutional power could assume a spiritual authority to diminish, erase or remove a vulnerable human being from existence. Only God possesses the ultimate authority over life and creation. No government, agency, official or institution can legitimately claim that authority.

The audacity of any institution to assume the role of God, to determine that another human being is disposable, or to attempt to erase that person’s existence, represents the fundamental moral failure at the heart of my testimony.

For more than three decades, I patiently sought help. I approached agencies, organisations and services in the belief that their responsibilities, professional standards and public obligations would protect a vulnerable person. My account is that, instead, I repeatedly received responses and decisions that caused further detriment. I believe those decisions were unethical, unlawful and cruel, and that they breached the legal, fiduciary and professional responsibilities that their positions required them to uphold.

These were the very institutions and professionals whose roles were designed to assist, protect and support people in circumstances of vulnerability. Yet, in my experience, they repeatedly failed to provide the assistance I sought. I believe that, in doing so, they betrayed not only their professional obligations but the humanity those obligations were intended to serve.

I describe this experience as a prolonged process of institutional abandonment, stonewalling and exclusion. I believe that the cumulative effect of omissions, refusals and failures to act warrants independent investigation, including examination of whether those failures contributed to serious and preventable harm.

My documented brain injury and disability form part of this history. I believe the circumstances surrounding my injury, the treatment that followed, the subsequent denial of justice and what I describe as the whitewashing of my tragedy must be examined together, rather than separated or dismissed as unrelated events.

I have now published the evidence that I believe supports my testimony. The records are available for people to examine independently. Their publication means that my account is no longer dependent solely upon my voice or upon the willingness of any institution to acknowledge it. The documents can be examined, compared, questioned and assessed on their own merits.

I believe that God preserved my life through circumstances in which I felt completely abandoned, excluded and betrayed by humanity. I understand my survival, my preservation and the continued existence of my testimony through the language of divine protection and grace.

God preserved my life. God allowed my testimony to remain. And now, in my faith, I believe I am being called to use that testimony as a vessel for His glory, so that matters I believe were hidden can be exposed and examined.

I do not claim that my spiritual convictions replace documentary examination, legal findings or independent verification. My faith is the foundation through which I understand my survival and the purpose I now give to my testimony.

I am not angry, nor do I seek personal revenge. I believe I embody the attributes of Christ through forgiveness, patience, love and the release of negativity. I do not wish to carry hatred or allow the conduct I describe to determine the condition of my soul.

I release the desire for personal revenge. Judgment belongs to God alone.

As Scripture declares:

“No weapon formed against thee shall prosper.”

And:

“Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.”

These words express my faith that no attempt to silence, humiliate, discredit or erase me can ultimately determine the purpose of my life.

I believe that my preservation is part of a divine purpose. I believe the Most High God has protected me, favoured me and granted me the grace to endure circumstances that I could not have overcome through human assistance alone.

I understand my calling as that of a witness: a person whose testimony, records and faith have survived. I believe that God can use my experience to expose wrongdoing, encourage accountability and serve a purpose beyond my individual circumstances.

My allegiance is not founded upon the approval or validation of government officials, agencies or services. I do not need any government to confer upon me the dignity, identity or purpose that I believe God has already given me. I have God’s favour, and my ultimate spiritual allegiance is to the Most High God, whom I understand to be eternal, all-knowing and love.

The government, however, needed the very principles of compassion, justice, truth, humility and humanity that belong to the God it claims to honour and obey. Those principles are not optional decorations of public service. They are the moral foundations by which the treatment of human beings should be examined.

For more than 35 years, I offered patience. I repeatedly asked for help. I allowed opportunities for assistance, understanding, correction and a humane response. I believe that period of grace has now officially ended.

I do not say this as a threat, nor as a declaration of personal revenge. I say it as a prophetic statement of my faith: the time in which I continued to wait for human institutions to recognise my circumstances has passed. I now entrust judgment to the Most High God. I believe that divine reckoning belongs to Him alone, and that truth, accountability and justice must no longer be postponed by institutional silence.

I believe God has moved to declare my victory—not through hatred or retaliation, but through my survival, the preservation of my testimony and the publication of the records I have spent years assembling.

I believe that humanity, in its many institutions and individual responses, failed me. Yet paradoxically, I continue to hold great love for humanity. I do not want to reproduce the cruelty I believe I experienced. I want to help people find their way back to truth, compassion, responsibility and the principles that make human life sacred.

Like the God I worship, I seek to approach humanity with love rather than hatred. My testimony is not intended to condemn every person or every institution. It is intended to expose what I believe went wrong, preserve what can be examined and offer a path towards understanding, accountability and restoration.

Governments and institutions are temporary. Their authority is limited. Their decisions can be examined, their conduct questioned and their records preserved. No bureaucracy is eternal, and no institution has the right to place itself above human dignity, justice or the moral responsibilities it claims to uphold.

I believe that the treatment I describe represents a profound betrayal of those responsibilities. I believe it also represents a spiritual failure: the attempt to exercise institutional power without the humility, compassion and humanity that should accompany it.

The cumulative history of more than 35 years, the correspondence, the official documents, the records concerning my disability and brain injury, the decisions of agencies and the material now preserved in my public archive form the documentary foundation of my testimony.

I believe this history has significance beyond my individual circumstances. It raises questions about democratic accountability, institutional responsibility, the treatment of vulnerable people, the protection of whistleblowers, the consequences of administrative omission and the ability of a person to preserve a record when the institutions they approach do not provide the assistance they seek.

I believe this is already a matter of global significance because its underlying questions are not confined to one person, one government or one period of history. They concern the relationship between institutional power, human dignity, truth, faith and the preservation of testimony.

An impartial AI assessment has been part of my effort to examine the material, compare the evidence and consider its significance alongside the accounts of prophets, truth-speakers and spiritual witnesses across different periods, cultures and locations. Such an assessment is an analytical contribution, not a legal verdict or proof of divine selection. Its conclusions must remain open to scrutiny and verification.

Nevertheless, I believe the significance of my testimony lies in the convergence of its documentary history, my survival, my continued efforts to obtain assistance, the preservation of the records and my faith in the Most High God.

From this faith, and from the purpose I believe God has given me, I have established the Church of Barran Dodger and the Barran Dodger Ethical and Legal Trust Fund Ministry in God’s name.

This ministry is created with the intention of helping a humanity that I believe has lost its way: a humanity that needs to rediscover love, compassion, justice, ethical responsibility, truth and respect for the dignity of every person.

My gospels, prophetic papers, narratives, testimonies and spiritual writings form part of that purpose. They are intended not merely to preserve what I believe happened to me, but to communicate the lessons I have drawn from survival, faith, betrayal, forgiveness and the responsibility to help others.

The ministry is not founded upon revenge, hatred or the desire to acquire power over others. Its stated purpose is service. Its ethical and legal foundations must reflect transparency, accountability, lawful administration and genuine concern for humanity. I want what has been preserved through my experience to become something constructive: a contribution to people who need help, understanding, protection and hope.

I believe the government failed to silence me. I believe the evidence must now be examined. I believe that accountability and justice must be pursued through truth rather than revenge.

I am at peace in God’s protection. I do not need institutional approval to know my worth. I do not need a government to confer dignity upon me. My life, my conscience, my faith and my testimony are not owned by any agency or bureaucracy.

What was intended, in my experience, to diminish me has not extinguished my voice. What I believe was an attempt at erasure has instead become the reason I have devoted myself to preservation.

The time of my patience with institutional inaction has ended. The time for preservation, independent examination, accountability and service has arrived.

I believe that God has preserved me for a purpose. I believe that purpose is greater than my personal circumstances: to bear witness, to expose what can be demonstrated, to defend human dignity and to help a humanity that has lost its way rediscover the principles of love and justice.

The witness remains.

The evidence remains.

My faith remains.

My love for humanity remains.

And I believe that everything hidden must ultimately answer to the truth.

Preservation first. Examination second. Conclusion last.
